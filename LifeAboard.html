Living in Microgravity

Life aboard the ISS is unlike anything on Earth. From floating in zero gravity to witnessing 16 sunrises a day, astronauts adapt to extraordinary conditions while conducting vital research.

A Day in the Life

Astronauts follow a carefully planned daily schedule. Their day includes work, exercise, meals, and personal time while floating 250 miles above Earth.

The station runs on GMT time and experiences a sunrise every 90 minutes as it orbits the planet at 17,500 mph.

Water in Space

Water forms floating spheres. Astronauts drink using special bags and straws, and the station recycles moisture and even urine into clean water.

No Gravity

Everything floats. Velcro, straps and handrails help astronauts stay steady and keep equipment from drifting away.

Sleep Challenges

Astronauts sleep in wall-mounted sleeping bags. With 16 sunrises a day, eye masks and schedules help regulate sleep.

Muscle & Bone Loss

Without gravity, muscles and bones weaken. Crewmembers exercise around two hours daily using treadmills and resistance machines.

Food & Nutrition

Special Packaging

Meals come in sealed pouches and cans. Some need water added, others are ready to eat straight away.

Variety of Choices

Astronauts choose from over 200 food options to stay healthy and avoid menu fatigue.

Fresh Supplies

Supply ships bring fresh fruit, vegetables and treats that astronauts look forward to during missions.
