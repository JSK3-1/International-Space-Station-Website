History of the International Space Station

Timeline

1984 — President Reagan directs NASA to build a space station and calls for international cooperation.

1993 — Russia joins the project, merging U.S. Freedom plans with the Russian Mir-2 design.

1998 — Zarya, the first ISS module, launches from Kazakhstan.

2000 — Expedition 1 arrives, beginning continuous human presence in space.

2011 — Final assembly is completed with the last shuttle mission delivering key components.

The International Space Station

The ISS represents the largest international collaboration in human history. From idea to orbit, it shows how nations worked together to build a home in space.

A Global Achievement

The ISS is a symbol of peaceful cooperation, built by five space agencies representing 15 countries. Construction began in 1998 and lasted over 13 years, requiring more than 40 assembly flights and over 1,000 hours of spacewalks. It has been continuously inhabited since November 2000, making it the longest human presence in low Earth orbit.
